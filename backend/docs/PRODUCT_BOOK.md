# Product Book — Prayer Suggestion API

**Audience:** an AI coding agent implementing this from scratch, plus the human reviewing it.
**Status:** specification. Nothing in `api/` or `bench/` exists yet.
**Prerequisite that already exists:** `data/build/datasets/` (three extracted sources plus a cross-source link table) and `src/prayer/extract/` (the extractors that produce it). See §3.

Read this whole document before writing code. Build the milestones **in order**; each one ends in a working, demonstrable system. Do not start a milestone until the previous one's Definition of Done passes.

---

## 1. What we are building

An HTTP API that takes a described life situation and returns, for each suggested biblical prayer:

1. the biblical prayer itself (verbatim scripture, with reference),
2. **instructions** — how to pray it, why it fits,
3. **a ready-to-speak prayer** — first-person words the user can say aloud right now.

Item 3 is the product. Instructions alone are not enough. If a response contains guidance but nothing the user can actually pray, that response has failed.

### Worked example

**Request:** `{"situation": "I've been trying for a child for four years and I'm losing hope."}`

**Response (one suggestion, abbreviated):**

> **Hannah Prays for a Son** — 1 Samuel 1:11 (WEB)
> *Solitary · Petition, Oath*
>
> **Passage:** "Yahweh of Armies, if you will indeed look on the affliction of your servant and remember me, and not forget your servant, but will give to your servant a boy, then I will give him to Yahweh all the days of his life..."
>
> **Why this fits:** Hannah prayed out of years of unanswered longing for a child. Her prayer does not hide the bitterness — it brings it directly to God.
>
> **How to pray it:** Pray this alone and out loud, unhurried. Say the actual number of years. Hannah bargained; you don't have to, but you may be as blunt as she was.
>
> **Pray this:**
> "Lord of Hosts, you see what no one else sees.
> Look on the affliction of your servant, and remember me.
> Four years I have waited, and hope is running out of me.
> I ask you for a child. I ask you plainly, because you are the one who gives.
> And where I cannot see, hold me anyway.
> Amen."

Note what the spoken prayer does: it opens with an address, echoes a **verbatim phrase** from the passage ("look on the affliction of your servant, and remember me"), names the user's own situation in first person, asks, and closes. That five-part shape is specified in §5 and is the contract every composer implements.

> **The passage text above is illustrative and unverified.** No translation is bundled in this repo yet, so it was written from memory to show the response shape. Replace it with the real WEB text once M1 lands, and never treat it as a fixture until then. Constraint C1 exists precisely because approximate scripture is easy to produce and hard to spot.

### Out of scope for v1

Multi-turn conversation; user accounts, history, or saved prayers; audio/TTS; non-English; sources beyond those in `data/build/datasets/`; theological Q&A; any hosted LLM call at request time.

## 2. Non-negotiable constraints

These are product requirements, not preferences. Violating any of them is a bug.

| # | Constraint | Why |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Scripture is never paraphrased.** `passage_text` and any `anchor` movement are byte-exact substrings of the bundled translation. | Fabricated scripture is the worst failure this product can have. |
| C2 | **No hosted LLM at request time.** All inference is local. | User decision. Hosted SDKs may still be used *offline* for building eval fixtures. |
| C3 | **The API runs fully offline** after build. No network in the request path. | Follows from C2 and the bundled translation. |
| C4 | **Every response is either a complete suggestion or an explicit abstention.** Never a partial or empty success. | A silently degraded pastoral answer is worse than "I don't have anything close." |
| C5 | **Composition never fails the request.** Every composer has a deterministic fallback path. | The endpoint must not 500 because a local model was slow or emitted bad JSON. |
| C6 | **Bundled text must be public domain** and redistributable. | Licensing; see §4. |
| C7 | **The safety gate runs on every request**, before retrieval, regardless of configuration. | §7. |

### What the later sources change

The spec below was written when Parks was the only corpus. Lockyer does not
replace it — it fills exactly the gaps Parks has, and three of those bear on
milestones already specified. **These are proposals for the human to accept or
reject, not settled scope:**

1. **M1 (text layer) gets a fallback.** Lockyer supplies 378 KJV quotations that a
   pastor hand-picked as the representative verse of each prayer. That is a
   curated `anchor` candidate — better than any window we would select
   automatically — and it covers the 3 Maccabees gap for nothing. WEB is still
   needed for full passages; this is an addition, not a substitute.
2. **M2 (retrieval) gets a real situation vocabulary.** Lockyer's titles are
   already situation-shaped — *Prayer for an Unborn Child*, *Prayer of a
   Discouraged Heart*, *Prayer in Time of War*, *Prayer in Calamity* — which is far
   closer to how a user describes their life than Parks' 16 abstract content
   labels. Index `derived.theme` and `derived.situation_terms` as high-weight BM25
   fields for any Parks record with a link.
3. **M2/M5 (phrase bank) gets seed material.** 685 first-person-plural
   "application sentences" are Lockyer addressing the reader about their own life
   — the exact register the `naming`, `ask`, and `trust` movements need. Seed the
   phrase bank from these instead of hand-writing 64 snippets.

**Constraint C6 needs care here.** Lockyer's exposition and poetry are in
copyright (© 1959 Zondervan) and must not be served verbatim by the API or
included in anything published. His KJV quotations are public domain and are
safe. Using the application sentences as *style* guidance for a local model is a
grey area the human should rule on before M5 — see §11.

**Watters 1883 changes the licensing picture entirely.** It is public domain, so
its text *and* its taxonomy may be shipped and redistributed — the only source in
the repo of which that is true. Three further proposals, again for the human to
accept or reject:

4. **M1 (text layer) has a public-domain KJV source on hand.** 1,990 citations
   carry inline scripture. This still does not replace bundling WEB — 126k words
   of excerpts is not a Bible, and the archaic English of the KJV measurably
   hurts embedding retrieval and reads oddly aloud. Use it as a fallback and a
   cross-check, not as the primary translation.
5. **M2 (retrieval) gets a real taxonomy instead of a hand-written lexicon.**
   `situation_lexicon.yaml` was specified as something you would author from
   scratch. Watters supplies 754 hand-built topics on three axes that map onto a
   situation query almost directly: what is needed (`facet: object`, 730
   citations), the inner state (`spiritual_condition`, 291), and the circumstance
   (`outward_condition`, 289). Seed the lexicon from these.
6. **The `instructions` half of the response has a source.** Facets `hindrance`
   (what may be blocking prayer), `answer_mode` / `answer_timing` /
   `answer_extent` (what kind of answer to expect, including "delayed" and "heard
   but not granted"), and `ground` (193 citations on what a person may appeal to)
   are directly usable — and `ground` feeds the `address` and `trust` movements.

**Do not treat facets as a filter that hides chapters.** Every citation keeps
`chapter_n` and `chapter_title` verbatim; `facet` is a derived convenience. All
30 chapters carry product-relevant material, and no chapter is a residual bucket.

Corpus shape you must design around:

- **43 of 224 records are Psalms** and they are the most thematically generic. Without correction they will dominate top-k for nearly every query. Measure this (`psalm_share@5`) from M3 onward.
- **94 records are a single verse.** A one-verse anchor is thin material for composing a prayer. Composers must handle this — see §5.4.
- **2 records exceed 30 verses** (Ps 89 is 52). Never return a 52-verse passage inline; see §5.4.
- **1 record has `verse_count: null`** (`Bar 2:11–3:8`, crosses a chapter boundary).
- **3 records cite parallel gospel accounts** (`n_refs` > 1). The ranges are the same prayer; do not concatenate them.
- **7 records carry `imprecation` or `curse`.** These need a compose policy — §7.3.

## 4. The text layer (Milestone 1 — build this first)

The dataset has references, not text. **Nothing else in this product can be built or evaluated until text resolution works.**

**Translation:** World English Bible (WEB), public domain. Source it from ebible.org. You need an edition that includes the deuterocanon/apocrypha to cover the 34 DC records.

**Known gap to resolve, not assume:** 3 Maccabees (2 records) and 4 Maccabees (1 record) are absent from most Western apocrypha editions, including the KJV 1611 Apocrypha.

> `parks2021.0190` — 3 Mac 2:2–20 — The Jews Pray for the Lord's Help
> `parks2021.0191` — 3 Mac 6:2–15 — Eleazar Prays for Protection
> `parks2021.0192` — 4 Mac 6:27–29 — Eleazar Prays for God's Mercy

Try WEB-with-deuterocanon, then Brenton's Septuagint (public domain) for 3 Maccabees. **Do not fabricate, machine-translate, or paraphrase to fill a gap.** If a record's text cannot be sourced, set `text_available: false`, exclude it from retrieval by default, and record it in `data/build/text/COVERAGE.md`. Three missing records out of 224 is an acceptable v1 outcome; one invented verse is not.

**Deliverable:** `data/build/text/web.jsonl`, one row per `prayer_id`:

```json
{"prayer_id": "parks2021.0037", "translation": "WEB", "text_available": true,
 "refs": [{"osis": "1Sam.1.11", "display": "1 Samuel 1:11",
           "verses": [{"osis": "1Sam.1.11", "n": 11, "text": "..."}]}],
 "full_text": "...", "word_count": 61}
```

It lands in `data/build/text/`, beside the datasets but separately regenerable. Build it with `make text`, deterministic and re-runnable like the other steps.

**Definition of Done:** 221+/224 records resolve; `data/build/text/COVERAGE.md` lists every gap with the reason; a CLI (`python -m prayer.extract.text --show parks2021.0037`) prints a passage; every resolved verse count matches the dataset's `verse_count` where that field is non-null.

## 5. Architecture

### 5.1 Principle

Two independent pluggable axes, chosen per request:

```
situation ──▶ [SafetyGate] ──▶ [SituationAnalyzer] ──▶ [Retriever] ──▶ [TextResolver] ──▶ [Composer] ──▶ response
                                       │                  R1 | R2 | R3                      F1 | F2 | F4
                                       └── labels, person, intent
```

Every stage is a Protocol with a registry. Adding a retriever or composer later must require **no changes** to the API layer, the request/response models, or the benchmark harness — only a new module and a registry entry. This is the modularity requirement; treat a change that breaks it as a design failure.

### 5.2 Layout

```
api/
  __init__.py
  app.py                 FastAPI app, routes, error handling
  config.py              pydantic-settings; configs/base.yaml + env override
  registry.py            @register("retriever", "bm25") decorator + lookup
  models.py              ALL request/response Pydantic models
  corpus.py              loads the dataset + resolved text into memory once at startup
  pipeline.py            orchestrates the stages; owns fallback logic
  safety.py              SafetyGate
  analyze.py             SituationAnalyzer
  retrievers/
    base.py              Retriever Protocol, Candidate
    bm25.py              R1
    dense.py             R2
    hybrid.py            R3
  composers/
    base.py              Composer Protocol, Composition, movement kinds
    deterministic.py     the fallback path — no model, always works
    phrasebank.py        F1
    schema.py            F2
    free.py              F4
    verify.py            shared verifier used by F2 and F4
    phrases/*.yaml       the phrase bank (data, not code)
  llm/
    base.py              LocalLM Protocol
    transformers_lm.py   local model, lazy-loaded, timeout, warmup
    null.py              raises immediately — used to test fallback paths

docs/PRODUCT_BOOK.md     this file
```

### 5.3 Interfaces

Define these in M0 and do not change them casually — every later milestone depends on their stability.

```python
class Retriever(Protocol):
    name: str
    def retrieve(self, q: AnalyzedSituation, k: int, filters: Filters) -> list[Candidate]: ...

class Composer(Protocol):
    name: str
    def compose(self, q: AnalyzedSituation, p: PrayerRecord, passage: Passage) -> Composition: ...

class LocalLM(Protocol):
    def generate(self, prompt: str, *, max_tokens: int, timeout_s: float,
                 schema: type[BaseModel] | None = None) -> str: ...
```

`Candidate` carries `prayer_id`, `score`, and `matched_on: list[str]` — the human-readable reasons this record surfaced. `matched_on` is required, not optional: it powers both explainability in the response and debugging in the benchmark.

### 5.4 The spoken prayer: movement structure

**This is the core contract of the product.** A composed prayer is an ordered list of movements. All three composers produce this same structure; they differ only in how the text of each movement is produced.

| Kind | Required | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `address` | yes | How God is addressed. Drawn from the passage's own address if it has one. |
| `anchor` | yes | **Byte-exact substring of `passage_text`** (C1). Carries `verbatim_from` = the OSIS ref. |
| `naming` | yes | The user's situation, first person, in the user's own terms. |
| `ask` | yes | The petition. Must align with the record's `contents` labels — see §7.3 for the imprecation exception. |
| `trust` | no | Confidence/surrender. Omit where the source prayer has none (laments often don't). |
| `close` | yes | "Amen." or a scripture-derived closing. |

Hard rules, enforced by `composers/verify.py` and tested:

- **`anchor` must appear verbatim in `passage_text`.** Non-negotiable. If a composer cannot produce a valid anchor, it fails over to the deterministic composer.
- **Total spoken prayer: 60–180 words.** Long enough to be a real prayer, short enough to say in one breath-cycle. Report `word_count` and `read_time_seconds` (≈150 wpm).
- **First person singular** by default; switch to intercessory ("we", "them") when the analyzer detects the situation is about someone else.
- **Never quote scripture outside the `anchor` movement.** Other movements are the composer's own words. This keeps "what is scripture" and "what is generated" cleanly separable for the user and for the verifier.

Handling the corpus edges:

- **Single-verse records (94 of them):** the anchor may be the whole verse; lean on the record's `contents` labels and `speaker` for the rest.
- **Long records (>20 verses):** select an anchor window rather than quoting the whole passage. Return the full passage in `passage_text` but mark `passage_truncated: true` and include a `passage_excerpt` of ≤ 8 verses centered on the anchor.
- **Multi-ref records:** use `refs[0]` (the primary) for the anchor; list the parallels in the reference display.

### 5.5 Composers

All three implement the same `Composition` output. Build them in the order F1 → F2 → F4.

**`deterministic`** (not user-selectable; the fallback for all others). Jinja2 templates + the phrase bank, zero model. Always produces a valid `Composition`. Its quality is the floor of the entire product, so make it genuinely decent — this is what users get whenever a model is slow, absent, or wrong.

**`phrasebank` (F1).** Deterministic assembly, then a small local model smooths transitions and adapts the `naming` movement to the user's wording. The model may **rewrite** movements but may not **introduce** content, and may never touch `anchor`. Cheapest real generation; lowest risk.

**`schema` (F2).** The model fills a Pydantic schema, one field per movement. Validate → on failure retry (max 2) → on exhaustion fall back to `deterministic`. Report `fallback_used` and `retry_count` in provenance.

**`free` (F4).** The model writes the prayer freely given the passage, labels, and situation; `verify.py` then gates it. Best prose, highest risk — a small local model will fabricate confidently. The verifier catches quote and citation violations but **cannot** catch a plausible-sounding false theological claim. Ship this behind a config flag defaulting to off, and say so in the docs.

## 6. API contract

```
POST /suggest
GET  /prayers/{prayer_id}
GET  /health
GET  /config          → available retrievers, composers, translations
```

**Request:**

```json
{
  "situation": "string, 3–1000 chars, required",
  "k": 3,
  "retriever": "bm25 | dense | hybrid",
  "composer": "phrasebank | schema | free",
  "canon": ["OT", "DC", "NT"],
  "translation": "WEB",
  "include_passage_text": true
}
```

`retriever` and `composer` default from `configs/base.yaml`. `canon` lets callers exclude the deuterocanon — a real need for Protestant users; default to all three.

**Response:**

```json
{
  "query": {"situation": "...", "k": 3, "retriever": "hybrid", "composer": "schema"},
  "safety": {"status": "ok", "notice": null},
  "abstained": false,
  "suggestions": [{
    "prayer_id": "parks2021.0037",
    "title": "Hannah Prays for a Son",
    "reference": {"osis": "1Sam.1.11", "display": "1 Samuel 1:11", "translation": "WEB"},
    "passage_text": "...",
    "passage_truncated": false,
    "labels": {"context": "solitary", "contents": ["petition", "oath"],
               "speaker": "Hannah", "canon_section": "OT"},
    "match": {"score": 0.71, "matched_on": ["petition", "longing", "waiting"]},
    "instructions": {"why_it_fits": "...", "how_to_pray": "...", "posture": "..."},
    "spoken_prayer": {
      "text": "full prayer, newline-joined, ready to read aloud",
      "movements": [{"kind": "anchor", "text": "...", "verbatim_from": "1Sam.1.11"}],
      "word_count": 96, "read_time_seconds": 38
    },
    "provenance": {"composer": "schema", "model": "...", "fallback_used": false,
                   "retry_count": 0, "latency_ms": 412}
  }],
  "timings": {"total_ms": 480, "retrieval_ms": 12, "composition_ms": 455}
}
```

**Abstention (C4).** When the best score is below `abstain_threshold`, return `abstained: true`, an empty `suggestions`, and a `message`. With 224 prayers, many real situations — a layoff, IVF, addiction recovery — have no close biblical analogue. Returning the nearest generic psalm for everything is the failure mode this guards against. Tune the threshold in M8 against Tier 3; until then set it conservatively low and log near-misses.

## 7. Safety and pastoral policy

### 7.1 Crisis gate (implement in M2, not later)

`safety.py` runs before retrieval on every request. It detects situations involving suicidal ideation, self-harm, abuse, or violence toward others.

On detection: still return a prayer — a person in crisis asking for prayer should not be met with a blank refusal — but prepend `safety.notice` with real-world signposting (crisis line guidance appropriate to an unspecified locale), select from `lament` / `petition` / `complaint` records, and **suppress the `free` composer**, forcing `deterministic` regardless of the requested composer. Never let a small local model improvise words to a person in crisis.

Implement deterministically (keyword + pattern lists, versioned in `api/safety_terms.yaml`) so it is auditable and testable. Accept false positives over false negatives.

### 7.2 The corpus is not a mirror of the user

The dataset includes prayers by hostile or ambiguous figures — the prophets of Baal (1 Ki 18:26), Antiochus IV, a proud Pharisee held up as a *negative* example (Lk 18:11). Composing an earnest first-person prayer out of the Pharisee's self-righteous boast would be a serious failure.

Add a `compose_policy` field to a curated overlay file (`policy/compose_policy.yaml`), keyed by `prayer_id`, with values:

- `compose` — normal (the default for the great majority)
- `explain_only` — retrieve and explain, but no spoken prayer; the response says why
- `exclude` — never retrieve

Seed it with: the Baal prayer, the Pharisee's prayer, Antiochus, and the imprecatory set below. The human reviewer signs off on this file; the agent proposes it. Keep it as data, not code.

### 7.3 Imprecatory prayers

Seven records carry `imprecation` or `curse`:

> `0036` Jdg 21:18 · `0085` Ps 10 · `0088` Ps 17 · `0095` Ps 35 · `0136` Je 18:19–23 · `0137` Je 20:7–18 · `0140` Je 51:34–35

These are real prayers and belong in the corpus — Psalm 35 is exactly right for someone being treated unjustly. But the `ask` movement must **not** compose a request for harm against a person the user has named. Policy: for records with `imprecation`/`curse`, the `ask` movement asks God for justice, vindication, and rescue, and explicitly leaves judgment to God. The instructions may explain what the psalmist asked for; the spoken prayer does not put it in the user's mouth. Encode this as a composer rule with dedicated tests.

## 8. Milestones

Each milestone is a working system. Do not proceed past a failing DoD.

### M0 — Skeleton
Repo layout, `config.py`, `registry.py`, all Protocols and Pydantic models, FastAPI app with `/health` and `/config`. No retrieval or composition logic.
**DoD:** `uvicorn api.app:app` serves; `/config` lists registered (empty) components; `pytest` green; adding a stub retriever requires touching only its own file plus one registry line.

### M1 — Text layer
§4. `src/prayer/extract/text.py`, `data/build/text/web.jsonl`, `data/build/text/COVERAGE.md`.
**DoD:** §4's DoD.

### M2 — Minimal working product: R1 + deterministic composer + safety gate
BM25 over a synthetic document per prayer (title + context/content labels + label definitions + speaker + place + passage text). `SituationAnalyzer` v1: keyword→content-label mapping (`policy/situation_lexicon.yaml`), first/third person detection. Deterministic composer with the phrase bank. Crisis gate (§7.1) and compose policy (§7.2).
**No model of any kind runs in this milestone.**
**DoD:** `POST /suggest` returns a schema-valid response with a real spoken prayer for 20 hand-written situations in `tests/fixtures/situations.jsonl`; anchor-verbatim invariant holds for all 20 (assert it); crisis fixtures trigger the gate; `explain_only` records return no `spoken_prayer`; p95 latency < 100 ms. **This is the version to demo and react to before building anything else.**

### M3 — Benchmark harness + Tier 1
`prayer.bench.run` executes a retriever × composer matrix and writes `data/build/bench/results/<ts>/report.md`. Tier 1 known-item queries (§9).
**DoD:** one command reproduces a report; M2's numbers are recorded as the baseline every later milestone is compared against; harness discovers components from the registry rather than a hardcoded list.

### M4 — R2 dense + R3 hybrid
Local embeddings via onnxruntime (`bge-small-en-v1.5` or `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`), 224 vectors precomputed at build time into `data/build/index/`. Numpy exhaustive search — **no vector database**. R3 = reciprocal rank fusion of R1+R2.
**DoD:** all three selectable; Tier 1 metrics for all three in one report; index build is deterministic and cached; retrieval p95 still < 50 ms.

### M5 — Local LLM runtime + F1 (`phrasebank`)
`llm/transformers_lm.py`: lazy load, warmup at startup, hard timeout, thread-safety. `llm/null.py` for fallback testing.
**DoD:** F1 selectable; forcing `null.py` produces deterministic output with `fallback_used: true` and never an error (C5); anchor invariant holds; p95 within the budget in `configs/base.yaml`.

### M6 — F2 (`schema`)
Schema-constrained generation, validate → retry ≤2 → fall back.
**DoD:** schema-valid rate and fallback rate reported over the full Tier 1 set; verbatim-quote rate exactly 100%; imprecation policy tests pass.

### M7 — F4 (`free`) + verifier
Free generation gated by `verify.py`. Config flag, default off.
**DoD:** verifier catches every violation in `tests/fixtures/adversarial_compositions.jsonl` (hand-written: fabricated quote, invalid citation, over-length, scripture outside the anchor, imprecation against a named person); docs state the residual risk plainly.

### M8 — Tier 3 gold set + full evaluation
60–80 curated situations with graded relevance, dev/test split. Full 3×3 matrix. Tune `abstain_threshold` and psalm diversification on **dev only**.
**DoD:** `data/build/bench/results/` contains a matrix report over Tier 3 test with a recommended default configuration and the evidence for it.

### M9 — Hardening
Rate limiting, response caching, request logging (situations are sensitive — log hashes, not text, by default), OpenAPI docs, README.

## 9. Benchmark

**Tier 1 (M3) — known-item retrieval.** Query = a paraphrase of each record's own title/descriptor; the correct answer is that record. n=224, free to build, generated by script from the dataset.

Tier 1 measures whether retrieval is *wired up correctly*. It does **not** measure whether the product is good — a system that aces Tier 1 can still be useless for real situations, because real users don't describe their circumstances in the vocabulary of prayer titles. Never present Tier 1 numbers as product quality. Its job is regression detection between M3 and M8.

**Tier 3 (M8) — curated.** 60–80 realistic situations in the human's own words, each judged against the corpus: 2 = ideal, 1 = acceptable, 0 = no. Split dev/test; never tune on test. Generating *candidate* situations offline with a hosted LLM is permitted (C2 governs the request path only) — but the **relevance judgments must be made by the human**, since they are the ground truth.

**Metrics.**

| Retrieval | Composition (all automatic) | System |
|---|---|---|
| Recall@1/5/10 | anchor-verbatim rate (must be 1.0) | latency p50/p95 |
| nDCG@10, MRR | schema-valid rate | fallback rate |
| label-precision@5 | citation-valid rate | abstention precision/recall |
| `psalm_share@5` | word count in 60–180 | tokens/sec (local model) |
| canon distribution | readability (`textstat`) | |
| | grammar errors (`language_tool_python`) | |

Automatic metrics cannot judge whether a prayer is *pastorally* right — the thing that actually matters. From M8, add blind human 1–5 ratings on ~50 sampled outputs for the finalist configurations. Budget for it.

## 10. Testing

- **Invariant tests** (highest value): anchor-verbatim, word-count bounds, movement completeness, no-scripture-outside-anchor, imprecation policy, `explain_only` suppression. Run these against **every** composer via a shared parametrized suite — that suite is what keeps the composers interchangeable.
- **Fallback tests:** inject `null.py`, model timeout, and malformed model output; assert a valid response with `fallback_used: true` every time.
- **Golden-file tests** for the deterministic composer (it must be reproducible).
- **Contract test:** a stub retriever and stub composer registered from a test module must work end-to-end without touching `api/` source. This is the executable definition of the modularity requirement.

## 11. Open decisions for the human

Do not guess these; raise them.

1. **Local model choice and size** for M5+. Trade-off is quality vs. latency vs. RAM. Recommend after measuring on the actual hardware.
2. **Sign-off on `compose_policy.yaml`** (§7.2) — the agent proposes, the human decides which prayers may be composed into first-person speech.
3. **Crisis-notice wording and locale** (§7.1) — the human writes this text; the agent must not invent crisis-line numbers.
4. **Whether the deterministic composer is publicly selectable**, or stays an internal fallback.
5. **WEB text redistribution** — WEB is public domain, but confirm before publishing the repo.
6. **How far Lockyer's exposition may be used** (§3). Serving it verbatim is out
   (still in copyright). Using the 685 application sentences as few-shot style
   material for a local composer is a judgment call that is yours, not the
   agent's. Decide before M5.
7. **Whether Lockyer enters the retrieval corpus in M2 or later.** Adding it grows
   the corpus from 224 to 568 records and materially improves the situation
   vocabulary, but it also means two record shapes in one index. Deferring to a
   post-M4 milestone keeps the baseline simpler.

## 12. Conventions

Python 3.12+, stdlib-first. Pydantic v2 for every boundary. Deterministic builds — same input, same bytes. Type hints throughout. Comments explain *why*, not *what* (match the style of `src/prayer/extract/parks.py`). Data that a human should be able to review — phrase banks, lexicons, policy, safety terms — lives in YAML under `policy/`, never hardcoded. Everything under `data/build/` is generated and reproducible; none of it is edited by hand, and none of it is in version control.
